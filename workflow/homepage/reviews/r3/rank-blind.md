## 4 — Sharpest criticism

**#1 (C):** No single piece of work is readable. The mosaic squeezes 8 tiles into a 576px column, each about 181px wide, so the proof reads as a logo wall. Grubhub is the only tile you can recognise as an ad in four seconds. Meanwhile about 160px of dead green sits between the text column (ends ~x670) and the mosaic (starts x832). The nav's "Book a demo" and "Sign in" pills also sit on top of the tiles at x1094–1328, y13–65. The fix: make one tile 2×2 (about 370px) as the hero piece.

**Median, #7 (D):** The headline is a ~90px book serif (Garamond-style), in title case, on warm grey, and one blue button is the only saturated color. That says law firm or quarterly review to a brief that specifies playful-bold. It gets worse because the only proof image comes in printer's crop marks, captioned "Source plate / customer-supplied automotive creative". The top-right nav slot, where the phone number or audit button belongs, holds "DEALERSHIP GROWTH EDITORS", which has no action.

## 5 — What separates the top two from the bottom two

C and I both give 30–34% of the fold to the business's actual output: client creative shown large enough that brand names or offers can be read. They tie it to one line saying who the service is for. And each puts a filled, high-contrast button within 600px of the headline's left edge. F and L both spend the fold on one abstract object (a 3D ribbon, a refraction prism) behind a claim with no object ("built for growth", "taking brands further"). They show 0% readable work. L has no action at all, and F's is a small dark-grey "CONTACT" pill in the nav corner. The top two present proof and then an action. The bottom two present a mood and then nothing.

The set as a whole is not weak: C, A, E, H and J are strong references.
