# Round 1 — rank

**Scoring note:** a reference cannot lose just because it isn't about car dealers. So each fold is scored on how well it would work for this buyer, a B2B buyer comparing agencies who trusts portfolio and converts by form or call, plus craft. Cookie banners are ignored.

## 1. Strict ranking

1. **E** — The fold states the service, shows nine real brand-work tiles (Grubhub, Roland, Colgate…) as proof, and gives one CTA. That is the only fold that answers what they do, proves it with work, and says what to do next, all in four seconds.
2. **L** — It shows five client-named work pieces (Moncler, Robinhood, Canva, National Theatre, OpenAI) at 54% of the fold, the strongest portfolio proof in the set. It has no service statement and no CTA, and a dead band about 180px tall at y≈120–300.
3. **K** — A category claim at 125px, a row of award logos (Cannes Lions, Webby, Drum, Ad Age) and two creator stills bleeding off the right edge. It matches the playful-bold register and names its category, but the proof is awards, not the work itself.
4. **D** — The only fold where a dealer sees recognizable dealer creative, the offer and two in-body CTAs at a glance. It loses points for decorative circuit-trace connector lines, a filler crosshair box, a generic condensed face, a wordmark with no nav links, and a stray blue tick at x≈1343, y≈810.
5. **C** — The best single photograph in the set, full-bleed with chalk overlay. But the only claim is a 36px line in the lower left ("Creative craft, designed to scale.") that names no service, and the only CTA is a nav link.
6. **F** — A full-bleed Nike piece captioned with the client name is strong proof for a portfolio buyer. There is no service statement and no action beyond a 12px scroll arrow.
7. **J** — It has everything (headline, pricing, seven creatives, phone, three audit CTAs), and that is the problem: about 14 objects of equal weight on the right half mean the eye has nowhere to land.
8. **B** — The positioning line is explicit (Shopify Plus partner for European fashion brands), but the card fan covers the headline, which reads as "We build the next" with the rest missing, so it looks broken.
9. **I** — Same copy and CTAs as D, but set in an editorial serif under a gold flyer, which is the wrong register. Internal notes leak into the fold ("Source plate / customer-supplied automotive creative", nav item "DEALERSHIP GROWTH EDITORS"), and the logo is pixelated and tiny.
10. **G** — One clear sentence and two work tiles. With a type ratio of about 1.4:1 and no CTA, it is quiet to the point of being inert for a playful-bold, form-converting buyer.
11. **H** — A 130px category shout over a generic 3D ribbon render. There is no work shown in the fold, so there is no proof.
12. **M** — Pure mood video with no client attached. The yellow 140px headline over pale lavender footage fails contrast where "CONNECT TO CULTURE" crosses the corridor.
13. **A** — The 190px slogan "TAKING BRANDS FURTHER" carries no checkable claim. The only image is a landscape cut off at y=665, and there is no CTA.

**Thirds:** top is 1–4, middle is 5–8, bottom is 9–13. This set is not weak. E, L, K, C and F are strong real work.

## 4. Sharpest criticisms

**#1, E:**
- **Headline:** "Your creative team's creative team™" (66px, mint on dark green) names no deliverable. The buyer only learns it means design production from the 3-line 18px subhead, so in four seconds they read a pun, not an offer.
- **Nav collision:** the masonry starts at y=0, so the "Book a demo" and "Sign in" pills (x≈1094–1328, y≈13–65) sit directly on the basketball and OPA tiles. The nav CTA fights the portfolio.
- **Fix:** replace the headline with the noun ("On-demand design and ad production"), and start the grid below y=80.

**Median #7, J:**
- **Price cards inside the portfolio:** the right 760px mixes four price cards into the creative masonry: LEAD GENERATION $2,500, LUXURY VIDEO $750, LIVE BDC AGENT TEAM $2,500, LEAD GEN + BDC TEAM $5,000. Each card is set in about 40px black type, so they compete with the ads. Two $2,500 cards of identical weight sit in column 1, and the portfolio reads as a rate card.
- **Cropping under the nav:** the top row is cut by the nav at y=72. The first tile shows an unreadable 110px-tall strip of a search-results screenshot.
- **CTA duplication:** "Get a Free Dealership Marketing Audit" appears twice (nav and body), plus a call button and the phone number in the nav. That is four actions on one fold.
- **Fix:** move all four price cards into one pricing strip below the fold, let the creatives fill all three columns, and start the grid below the nav.

## 5. Mechanism: top two vs bottom two

E and L each spend 30–54% of the fold on a grid of 5–9 separate work pieces, each labelled with a client name the buyer can check (Grubhub, Roland, Colgate; Moncler, Robinhood, OpenAI). The proof is counted, not claimed, and the variety between tiles shows range without a word of copy. Their largest type is 34–66px, so the work, not the slogan, is the biggest thing on the page. M and A do the opposite. They give their largest element (140–190px) to a slogan with nothing checkable in it ("Connect to culture. Spread joy." / "Taking brands further"). Their imagery is either not work at all (A's landscape, cut off at y=665) or mood footage with no client attached (M). Neither has a CTA in the body. The dividing mechanism is how many named work pieces are in the fold, multiplied by whether the biggest type makes a claim anyone could check. The bottom two score zero on both.

The three BDC folds (D, I, J) don't clear this test on its first half. D shows two creatives and I shows one, neither labelled with a dealership client, and J buries its creatives among price cards. Only D gets into the top third, and it gets there on offer clarity, not craft.
