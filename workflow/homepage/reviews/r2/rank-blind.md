# Round 2 — rank

**BDC Promotions: blind ranking of 13 desktop folds**

Buyer: a dealership GM comparing agencies. Conversion is the free audit plus a phone call. I read all 13 images and ignored cookie banners.

**1 — Strict ranking**

1. **I** – It is the only fold that answers all four questions a comparing dealer brings: what they do, proof in six real dealership ads, monthly prices with a disclosure line, and an audit button plus phone number. It wins on content, not craft.
2. **F** – It gives this buyer the fastest four-second read: a 95px condensed outcome headline, a 563×668 dealer ad, and stacked audit and call buttons, in the right bold register. Three things hold it back: decorative teal circuit-trace lines, no navigation at all, and a second ad cropped so it reads "MASSIV REPO SA".
3. **L** – This is the best-crafted version of the layout this buyer needs: headline on the left, one button, and a nine-tile work wall running off the top. But the line "your creative team's creative team" and the Grubhub and Colgate tiles tell a dealer it isn't for him, and there is no phone number.
4. **B** – It says the right thing to the right buyer, with a real ad and both calls to action. But a quiet editorial serif on beige contradicts the playful-bold brief. And "01 ——", the crop marks, "Source plate / customer-supplied" and "DEALERSHIP GROWTH EDITORS" are studio jargon a dealer can't read.
5. **M** – It has the strongest playful-bold typography in the set, and its award logos build trust. But it is pitched at influencer marketing, and about 45% of the blue panel between the headline and the awards strip is empty.
6. **A** – Top-tier craft: one full-bleed frame and one 62px caption. It tells a dealer only "they did Nike". There is no offer and no button, and the scale says "too big for you".
7. **J** – A beautiful photo-plus-chalk composite. The 36px tagline is shoved into a corner, there is no call to action, and the navigation is unreadable over the pale sky.
8. **E** – The portfolio grid proves the client roster. But a ~200px dead band under the navigation wastes 22% of the fold, and white titles on the pink and pale-green tiles fail contrast.
9. **C** – A poster-grade fold of type plus one 3D ribbon. "The global social agency built for growth" is a claim with no proof and no action.
10. **D** – The register is right but the execution is wrong. The 150px yellow headline sits over white and lavender video at about 1.1–1.5:1 contrast, and dancing influencers say nothing to a car dealer.
11. **G** – The big thin headline "Taking brands further" is a sentence any agency could sign. The one image starts at y665 and is cropped by the fold.
12. **H** – The only headline is physically covered by the fan of photos: "uild the" is hidden, and the sentence is unfinished anyway. The subhead is about Shopify fashion brands.
13. **K** – Nothing to look at first. The largest type is a 28px paragraph (1.4:1 ratio), there is no call to action, and the two tiles are abstract renders.

**4 — Sharpest criticism**

- **#1 (I):** The right half is chopped into 8 rectangles, and none of them is at least twice the size of the rest apart from the Luxury ad (413×516, only 16% of the fold).
  - The loudest shape on that side is the pricing box (~328×260, 2px black rounded border, bold $ figures around 26px). It takes the top-right slot, where the eye lands second, so the fold asks "$2,500 a month?" before it has shown the work.
  - Meanwhile the four event ads are shrunk to 156px wide. Their own headlines ("MASSIVE REPO SALE", "$0 DOWN") shrink to about 10px and can't be read, so the portfolio reads as texture, not proof.
  - Fix: show three ads at 300px wide or more, and move pricing below the fold or into a single line.
- **Median, #7 (J):**
  - The only statement, "Creative craft, designed to scale.", is about 36px light serif. It covers roughly 250×70px (about 1.4% of the fold) and sits 40px from the bottom-left corner.
  - The ~14px white nav sits on a pale lavender sky at roughly 1.5–2:1 contrast.
  - There is no call to action. The buyer gets a Louis Vuitton trunk, no named service and no way forward.

**5 — What separates the top two (I, F) from the bottom two (H, K)**

I and F share one mechanism:
- An outcome headline of 88px or more names the buyer's own goal ("your showroom") in the left 45% of the fold.
- The buyer's own kind of artifact — dealer ads with cars and dollar figures — fills 25% or more of the fold on the right.
- A filled audit button and a phone number are stacked within 120px under the subhead.

So every place the eye lands is the offer, the proof or the action.

H and K lack all three:
- H hides its only sentence behind an overlapping fan of photos, and its proof is fashion photography for another sector.
- K has no type larger than 28px and no call to action, and it leaves about 130px of white between the intro and the tiles, so the eye has no first place to land.
- Neither states an outcome or offers an action, which is why they finish last whatever their craft.
