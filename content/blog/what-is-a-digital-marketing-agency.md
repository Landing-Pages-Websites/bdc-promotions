<p>If you have asked, “what is a digital marketing agency?” the short answer is a company that plans or carries out marketing through digital channels. For a car dealership, the useful answer is more specific: a partner should connect the campaign, its message, the shopper’s next step, and the dealership’s follow-up into a process your team can understand and measure.</p><p data-mega-cta="primary"><a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/contact">Talk through your dealership’s marketing needs</a></p><h2>What is a digital marketing agency?</h2><p>A digital marketing agency helps organizations reach and engage audiences through online channels. The American Marketing Association describes digital marketing as marketing methods conducted through electronic devices, including online marketing efforts. The work can include planning, campaign setup, creative, channel management, measurement, or a combination of these responsibilities. (<a href="https://www.ama.org/what-is-digital-marketing/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">American Marketing Association’s digital marketing overview</a>.)</p><p>The term describes a broad category, not one fixed package. One agency may focus on paid advertising. Another may make websites, manage email programs, create social content, or advise on a mix of channels. Some agencies carry out the work; others focus on strategy or a limited service. A dealership should therefore ask what a particular provider will actually do, what the store must do, and how both sides will know whether the work is on track.</p><p>An agency is not automatically a replacement for dealership staff. Marketing can attract attention or start a conversation, but store teams still have important roles in confirming availability, answering questions, arranging next steps, and serving shoppers. The exact division depends on the written scope and the dealership’s own process.</p><h2>What should agency work accomplish for a dealership?</h2><p>Start with the business problem, not the platform. A dealership may want to introduce a model, promote an event, generate inquiries, or make its marketing more consistent. Those are different objectives. A clear goal helps determine the audience, message, channel, destination, follow-up plan, and measures that make sense.</p><p>Before speaking with providers, write down what the store wants a shopper to do. Examples include viewing current vehicle details, submitting an inquiry, calling the store, or requesting an appointment. Then identify who will respond and what should happen next. A campaign that produces responses but leaves ownership or follow-up unclear can create work without creating a useful customer experience.</p><p>Dealership marketing also has to fit real inventory and store priorities. The featured vehicles, offers, and wording should be checked by the dealership before a campaign goes live. Confirm how quickly details can change and who will tell the agency when the message needs an update. This practical coordination matters as much as the initial concept.</p><p>BDC Promotions describes its focus as helping dealerships generate more appointments and sell more cars. Its site presents an <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/process">overview of its campaign process</a> for prospects who want to understand the steps. Any provider conversation should still make clear which activities are included in the specific proposal being considered.</p><h2>Which parts of the work might an agency handle?</h2><p>Scope varies widely, so do not assume that a service is included because it is common in the industry. Ask the provider to define its responsibilities in plain language. The following categories can help a dealership organize the conversation.</p><ul><li><strong>Planning:</strong> Clarifying the intended audience, business objective, offer, message, channel, and destination.</li><li><strong>Campaign execution:</strong> Setting up or managing approved advertising and checking that the campaign runs as intended.</li><li><strong>Creative:</strong> Developing the images, video, copy, and calls to action that shoppers see.</li><li><strong>Destination and response:</strong> Reviewing where a shopper lands and how an inquiry is handed to the dealership for follow-up.</li><li><strong>Measurement:</strong> Reporting activity and outcomes against the agreed goal, with enough context to guide decisions.</li><li><strong>Communication:</strong> Sharing approvals needed, progress, changes, and next steps in a predictable way.</li></ul><p>These are categories to discuss, not a promise that every agency provides every service. Compare the written scope with the dealership’s needs. If you are reviewing BDC Promotions, its <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/services">services overview</a> and <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/work">examples of automotive advertising work</a> can help frame questions about its public materials. Confirm the precise deliverables, responsibilities, and exclusions directly before making a decision.</p><h2>How does dealership marketing differ from general digital marketing?</h2><p>The basic digital channels are not unique to auto retail, but the operating context is. A dealership has specific vehicles, changing availability, multiple models and price points, sales staff, and shopper questions that may need a timely response. A useful campaign has to reflect those realities instead of relying on generic copy that could fit any business.</p><p>For example, a campaign about a vehicle should use details the dealership has verified and a next step that is easy to understand. If inventory or offer information changes, the store needs a way to flag it. If a shopper responds, the dealership should know where the inquiry came from and who owns the follow-up. The agency and dealership should agree on these handoffs before launch.</p><p>Ask prospective partners how they learn the store’s priorities and how approvals work. A provider should be able to describe how it will gather information, show the proposed message and creative for review, and handle corrections. Dealership leaders should know whether they approve each asset, who can request changes, and how a change affects timing.</p><p>Industry focus can be useful when it leads to relevant questions and better-informed creative. But a label such as “automotive specialist” is not enough on its own. Look for evidence of thoughtful work, a clear process, and an ability to explain how the proposed campaign fits your actual dealership.</p><h2>How should you compare agency scope and channels?</h2><p>Compare providers on the work you need rather than counting services on a list. Two proposals may use similar terms while assigning very different tasks to the agency and the dealership. Ask each provider to map the work from planning through launch and follow-up.</p><table><thead><tr><th>Area to compare</th><th>Questions for the provider</th><th>What the dealership should clarify</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Objective and audience</td><td>What outcome is this campaign designed to support, and who is it intended to reach?</td><td>Which store priority, shopper group, and vehicle or offer are in scope?</td></tr><tr><td>Channel and setup</td><td>Which channel is proposed, and who configures and checks the campaign?</td><td>Who owns account access, approvals, and any needed store information?</td></tr><tr><td>Creative and review</td><td>What assets and copy will be created, and how are revisions handled?</td><td>Who reviews accuracy, brand fit, and final approval before launch?</td></tr><tr><td>Shopper handoff</td><td>Where does a response go, and how will its source be identified?</td><td>Who responds, what is the expected internal process, and how are issues escalated?</td></tr><tr><td>Reporting and changes</td><td>What will be reported, how often, and how are adjustments proposed?</td><td>Which measures matter, who reviews them, and who authorizes changes?</td></tr><tr><td>Cost and terms</td><td>What fees, media costs, duration, and exclusions apply?</td><td>What is the full commitment, how can scope change, and what happens at the end?</td></tr></tbody></table><p>Channel choice should follow the goal and the audience, not a promise that one platform is always best. Ask why the proposed channel fits the campaign, what role it plays, and how its results will be evaluated. If the provider recommends several channels, ask how each contributes and whether the team can support the added complexity.</p><p>Cost comparisons need equal care. Separate the agency’s fee from the advertising budget and any other expenses. Confirm the billing period, what is included, and whether costs can change with scope. Ask to see relevant examples and discuss how the provider will make a concept specific to your store. The message should be easy to understand, the visual should fit the subject, and the call to action should match the destination.</p><p>Agree on review before the work begins. A practical approval process names the person who can sign off, the method for sharing drafts, how feedback should be consolidated, and how much time is needed for revisions. No campaign asset should go live until the people authorized by the dealership have approved it. If a vehicle, price, or offer changes, establish how the creative will be checked again.</p><p>When reviewing examples, look beyond polish. Ask what the campaign was meant to do, what audience it addressed, what the viewer was asked to do, and how the work would be adapted for your store. BDC Promotions has a <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/testimonials">testimonials page</a> and a <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/about">company overview</a> that prospects can review as part of their own evaluation. Treat examples and testimonials as context, then ask for the scope and expectations that apply to your proposal.</p><h2>How should shopper inquiries and follow-up fit together?</h2><p>Advertising is one part of a customer journey. The dealership should understand where an inquiry will arrive, how it will be assigned, and what information the responding employee will see. Clarify whether the provider’s role ends when an inquiry is generated or includes any additional activity described in the agreement. Never infer responsibilities from a service label; get them stated clearly.</p><p>Before launch, test the path as a shopper would experience it. Check that the destination works on a phone, the form or contact method is usable, and the dealership knows how to access a submission. Confirm who checks for new inquiries, what happens outside normal business hours, and how duplicate or incomplete submissions are handled. These steps can reveal basic problems before they affect a live campaign.</p><p>Agree on a small set of operational measures as well as campaign measures. Depending on the agreed scope, these might include whether a page or form works, whether inquiry sources are visible, and whether the dealership can review the handoff. Do not treat an increase in raw inquiries as proof of business impact by itself. The store should interpret campaign activity alongside its own follow-up and sales process.</p><h2>Which results should an agency report?</h2><p>First decide what the report is meant to help you decide. A campaign report should connect its measures to the objective and explain any limits in the data. If the goal is to generate inquiries, ask for a clear account of inquiry activity and the relevant campaign context. If the purpose is awareness, the measures will differ. The provider should not present a number without explaining what it represents.</p><p>Ask for a consistent reporting rhythm and a short explanation of what changed since the last review. Useful discussions cover what was planned, what ran, what the available results show, what remains uncertain, and what action is proposed. The dealership should be able to see which decisions require its approval and what information the provider needs next.</p><p>Measurement depends on access and accurate setup. Ask who verifies tracking, how the dealership can view the agreed reports, and how questions are raised. If numbers differ across platforms or systems, request an explanation rather than assuming the figures mean the same thing. Agree on definitions and sources before judging performance.</p><p>BDC Promotions' <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/services/dealership-marketing-roi-calculator-5">dealership marketing ROI calculator page</a> is one resource a dealership can review while thinking about how to frame marketing questions. A calculation is only as useful as its inputs and assumptions. Ask a provider to make those assumptions visible and avoid treating a forecast as a guaranteed outcome.</p><h2>What should you prepare before a first agency conversation?</h2><p>A focused discussion saves time and helps both sides identify a realistic scope. You do not need a finished marketing plan, but gather the details that shape the work:</p><ul><li>The dealership’s immediate objective and the customer action you want to encourage.</li><li>The vehicles, offers, or store priorities that may be featured, with a process for verifying details.</li><li>The intended campaign area or audience, if you have one in mind.</li><li>The channels already in use and any access or approval requirements.</li><li>The person who will review creative, respond to questions, and make decisions.</li><li>The store’s current process for receiving and following up on inquiries.</li><li>The budget range and any timing, contract, or scope constraints that matter.</li></ul><p>Bring questions about deliverables, timing, fees, reporting, account ownership, and cancellation or renewal terms. Request a written proposal that distinguishes the agency’s work from the dealership’s responsibilities. If the proposal uses broad phrases such as “full service” or “lead generation,” ask for a list of concrete tasks and boundaries.</p><p>You can also compare the provider’s explanation with the dealership’s own priorities. BDC Promotions offers a <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/contact">contact page for discussing needs</a>, and its <a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/blog/automotive-dealership-marketing-plan-template">dealership marketing plan template</a> is another possible starting point for organizing questions. A conversation should help clarify fit, not pressure you to accept a scope you do not understand.</p><h2>How can a dealership choose a suitable partner?</h2><p>Use a short decision process. First, remove proposals that do not match the dealership’s objective or available resources. Next, compare the remaining options on scope, automotive relevance, creative review, inquiry handoff, reporting, communication, and total cost. Ask each provider the same core questions so differences are easier to spot.</p><p>Then check the working relationship. Will you know who to contact? Are the responsibilities clear? Does the proposed timeline allow the dealership to review materials? Can the provider explain its recommendations without making unsupported promises? A strong fit is not just a list of services; it is an arrangement both parties can execute and evaluate.</p><p>Finally, read the written agreement and proposal carefully. Confirm the work, fees, advertising spend, term, access, approvals, reporting, and exit process. Do not rely on an informal description if the contract says something different. Review its public information, compare it with your needs, and use a conversation to verify the specific service scope and terms relevant to your store.</p><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>Is a digital marketing agency the same as an advertising agency?</h3><p>The terms can overlap. An advertising agency may focus on creating and placing ads, while a digital marketing agency may cover advertising plus other online work. The labels are not standardized, so compare the actual deliverables instead of relying on the name.</p><h3>Does every digital marketing agency manage every channel?</h3><p>No. Providers differ in expertise and scope. Some specialize in one channel or service; others coordinate several. Ask which channels are included, who performs the work, what is excluded, and how each activity supports the agreed goal.</p><h3>Should a dealership hire an agency before setting its goal?</h3><p>It helps to identify a business priority first, even if the details are still open. A clear starting objective lets the dealership and provider decide whether a campaign is appropriate, what information is needed, and what success should mean. The agency can help refine the plan, but the dealership should approve the goal and scope.</p><h3>How can a dealership compare agency costs?</h3><p>Separate the service fee from media spend and other charges. Compare the same time period, deliverables, revision terms, reporting, and contract conditions. Ask the provider to state exclusions and explain how additional work is billed so that the quoted amount is understandable.</p><p data-mega-cta="secondary"><a href="https://bdcpromotions.com/contact">Learn more about dealership marketing with BDC Promotions</a></p><p>The right agency relationship starts with a clearly defined dealership need, a written scope, realistic measures, and an approval process your team can follow.</p>
