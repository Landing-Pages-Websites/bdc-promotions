<p>Dealership marketing rarely breaks down because no one bought an ad. More often, the handoff is unclear: a campaign creates interest, a lead waits for a response, and no one can explain how that activity became an appointment.</p><div class="answer-capsule"><p>An automotive marketing agency should connect paid social, prompt lead engagement, systematic follow-up, appointment generation, and reporting that makes each handoff visible. The goal is not simply more lead volume. It is a repeatable process for turning qualified shopper interest into conversations your team can work.</p></div><p>That is the standard owners, GMs, marketing leaders, and BDC directors should use when evaluating a partner. Start by tracing what happens from the first ad interaction through the next measurable buying step, then compare that workflow with the agency's actual capabilities and reporting.</p><h2>What Does an Automotive Marketing Agency Actually Connect?</h2><p>A dealership should not evaluate an automotive marketing agency by the number of ads it launches or leads it reports. The more useful question is whether the agency can connect the operating path that follows: paid social creates interest. A response process keeps the shopper engaged, and the dealership works toward a qualified appointment that can be tracked and reviewed.</p><p>That distinction matters because unclear return on investment, slow lead response, and low appointment conversion often occur between those steps, not inside the ad platform alone. A campaign may attract attention, but its value is difficult to assess if nobody can explain what happened after the form submission or message. The agency and dealership should agree on what counts as a lead, a meaningful conversation, a qualified opportunity, and an appointment.</p><h3>Paid social is the starting signal</h3><p>For BDC Promotions, the documented focus is Meta advertising for automotive companies, including campaign strategy and creative development.
